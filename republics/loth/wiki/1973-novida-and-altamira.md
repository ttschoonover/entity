For two years before Lothlórien, the North and South Houses were Novida and Altamira, home to one of Berkeley's best-known communes, the One World Family. The commune pooled its income, ran vegetarian restaurants, decided things at a family council, and left in 1975, the summer the co-op bought the houses.[^1][^2]

## Who they were
Allen Noonan was 51 when he founded the commune: an artist, café owner and UFO enthusiast from Britt, Iowa. He opened San Francisco's first vegetarian restaurant run entirely by commune members.[^1] He said extraterrestrials had contacted him in 1947 while he was painting a sign near Long Beach, and in 1970 he took the name Allen Michael.[^1][^3] His "Everlasting Gospel" mixed UFO beliefs, Christianity and New Age spirituality with an economic program he called Uni-Communism, and in 1973 he registered the commune as a church, the Universal Industrial Church of the New World Comforter.[^1]

Members held everything in common, drew no wages, and worked a daily five-hour shift they called "love service."[^1] The family council decided by consensus.[^4]

## The restaurant on Telegraph
![2455 Telegraph Avenue](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Amoeba_Music_Berkeley.jpg "2455 Telegraph Avenue in 2008, now Amoeba Music. From about 1970 to 1975 it was the One World Family Natural Foods and Entertainment Center.")

After San Francisco the commune opened restaurants in Marin and Berkeley.[^1] The Berkeley center, a big building at Telegraph and Haste, held a vegetarian restaurant, a pizzeria, the "Far Outfits" airbrushed-clothing shop and a performance hall, and the commune ran a bakery on the side.[^1][^5] It served 200 to 300 people a day.[^4] The building, 2455 Telegraph, is now Amoeba Music.[^5]

## Novida and Altamira
Early in 1973 the commune moved into the two old mansions on Prospect, empty former fraternity and sorority houses, and named them Novida and Altamira.[^1] It rented; it never had the money to buy property, and by 1975 it was shrinking and behind on rent.[^4] It left for Stockton, and that summer the University Students' Cooperative Association, now the BSC, bought both houses.[^2][^4]

## What carried over
- **The kitchen at the center.** The commune's whole economy was vegetarian food. The co-op opened the same buildings as its first all-vegetarian house, and house food is still vegetarian.[^2][^6]
- **Consensus.** The commune's family council decided by consensus; so does Loth's weekly council.[^4]
- **Work as a gift.** "Love service" was work for the group instead of for pay. Workshift is the same idea, written into a co-op's rules.[^1]
- **A commune, not just housing.** Loth has kept more of a commune's character than most BSC houses.[^7]

Loth didn't inherit the commune's beliefs. It inherited buildings that had already been run as one household.
