In the summer of 1975 the University Students' Cooperative Association, now the Berkeley Student Cooperative, bought the two houses on Prospect Street. That fall it opened them as Lothlórien, its first all-vegetarian house.[^1][^2]

## A co-op that bought old houses
The co-op began in 1933, in the Depression, when the Berkeley YMCA's director, Harry Kingman, helped a group of students start a house where workshifts paid for cheaper rent and shared food.[^3] For forty years it grew by taking over big buildings their owners no longer wanted.[^3]

The 1960s and 1970s were good years for that. Berkeley's Greek system was shrinking, and the USCA bought defunct fraternity and sorority houses that became Davis House, Andres Castro Arms and Wolf House.[^3][^4] The Prospect Street mansions had been fraternity and sorority houses from the 1920s to the 1960s as well.[^2] In the same decade the co-op took over Kingman Hall from the Berkeley Living Love Center, so Lothlórien was one of two houses it inherited from New Age communities in the 1970s.[^1][^3][^5]

## Vegetarian
The BSC's own history says it in one line: "In 1975, the Co-op opened its first all-vegetarian house, Lothlorien."[^1] The co-op had always fed its members from shared kitchens; a vegetarian house made the shared kitchen a statement. The houses' last residents had run vegetarian restaurants on Telegraph, and the Bay Area of the early 1970s was full of food co-ops and collective groceries.[^6][^7]

The rule still defines the house. House food is vegetarian, and the bylaws keep meat out of the common spaces.[^8] Plenty of elves are vegetarian or vegan and some aren't; the rule is about the commons.[^8]

## Elves
Lothlórien is the golden wood of the elves in *The Lord of the Rings*, and it suited two old houses in the trees at the foot of the hills.[^2] Residents have called themselves elves since the start.[^2] The house's customs mix folklore with house life, among them an initiation for new members each year and the Food Orgy, where residents make trays of finger food and feed each other without utensils.[^2][^5]

## The first years
The earliest photographs of the co-op house are Lee Altenberg's portraits of residents from 1978–1979, which he keeps online.[^9]

## Since then
- The vegetarian commons is the oldest rule in the house.[^1][^8]
- The house governs itself by consensus at a weekly council. Its constitution calls Loth "a vegetarian, consent-and-consensus-based cooperative."[^10]
- The co-op owns the buildings and the house runs itself, the split that began in 1975.
