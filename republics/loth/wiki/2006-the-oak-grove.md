From December 2006 to September 2008, protesters lived in the trees of the oak grove beside Memorial Stadium, a few blocks from the house, to stop the university from cutting it down for an athletic training center. It was one of the longest tree-sits on record. Elves were part of the protests, and the same spring students voted in a permanent fund for green projects at Cal.[^1][^2][^3]

## The grove
![The tree-sitters' camp in the oak grove, July 2008](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Berkeley_Oak_Grove_Tree-sitter_Camp.jpg "The tree-sitters' camp beside Memorial Stadium, July 8, 2008.")

The grove of coast live oaks stood along Piedmont Avenue below the stadium wall.[^1] It was the last stand of coast live oak woodland in the Berkeley lowlands, and it supported more than 300 animal species, among them acorn woodpeckers, squirrels and deer.[^1][^2] Berkeley protects coast live oaks above a certain size, and the site lay close to two shell mounds that UC Berkeley anthropologists had documented, with Ohlone burials.[^1][^4]

## The tree-sit
On December 2, 2006, four students climbed into the oaks.[^2] At the peak more than a dozen people lived in the trees, fed by volunteers on the ground.[^2] The Sierra Club and the California Native Plant Society opposed the project, and the City of Berkeley, neighbors and the California Oak Foundation sued, partly because the center sat on the Hayward Fault.[^1][^2][^5] An Alameda County judge stopped the tree removal in 2007.[^2]

In July 2008 the court ruled for the university, and campus police cleared the camps. In September a state appeals court refused to block construction. The grove came down over three days, and on September 9, 2008 the last four tree-sitters climbed down from a redwood and were arrested.[^2][^6]

## The Green Initiative Fund
On April 11–13, 2007, students voted on ASUC Bill 61a, which created The Green Initiative Fund: a student fee for projects that shrink the university's environmental footprint, awarded by a committee with a student majority. It passed with 69 percent.[^3][^7] Elves organized for it.[^8] The fee began at $5 a semester in fall 2007; students renewed it in 2016 and 2025, and it is now $15.[^3][^9]

## Since then
- Loth is known for its environmental and political activism.[^10]
- Any student can apply to TGIF for a sustainability grant, elves included. The house paid for its solar panels itself ([Living on the fault](#/wiki/history-2009-living-on-the-fault)); TGIF is where to ask for the next ones.[^7]
- The training center stands where the oaks did. The Hayward Fault the lawsuits argued about runs past Loth's back yard too.[^11]
