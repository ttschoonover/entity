*The minimal agreement every partner signs before admission. It is a draft by the founding partners, not legal advice: have it reviewed by a California attorney (and section 2.3 by a securities attorney) before money is contributed.*

## 1. Formation
1.1 The partners form a general partnership under the California Uniform Partnership Act of 1994 (Corporations Code section 16100 and following), named **$NAME** (the "Partnership"), with federal Employer Identification Number **$EIN**.
1.2 Its principal office is in $OFFICE. Because its name does not include every partner's surname, the Secretary files a fictitious business name statement with the Alameda County Clerk-Recorder within 40 days of the Partnership first transacting business, publishes it as the law requires, and renews it before it expires (Business and Professions Code section 17900 and following).
1.3 The Partnership begins on the date of its founding record ($FOUNDED) and continues until dissolved under section 15. Its tax year is the calendar year.

## 2. Purpose and limits
2.1 The Partnership exists so that its partners learn about investing by investing together, in publicly traded securities and funds, decided by the partners themselves.
2.2 The Partnership does not borrow, buy on margin, sell short, write options, trade futures, guarantee anyone's debt, or enter any contract or take any act that could make the partners liable beyond what they have contributed, or that could expose the Partnership or a partner to civil or criminal liability. It does not pay anyone for investment advice and no partner is paid for managing it.
2.3 The Partnership never offers interests to the public, advertises for partners, or admits a partner who will not take part in its decisions. It has at most 99 partners. The partners intend that, because every partner actively decides its investments, their interests are not securities; section 7 keeps it so.

## 3. The Record
3.1 The Partnership keeps its decisions, partners, offices, units, books and agreements on a signed record kept by the Athènes software (the "Record"), sealed so that only partners can read it.
3.2 Each partner agrees that signing on the Record with their own key is their electronic signature, with the same effect as a handwritten one (Civil Code section 1633.7), and that notices may be given through the Record and the Partnership's website.
3.3 Where the Record and this agreement differ about what was decided, the Record prevails; this agreement prevails over any setting of the software.

## 4. Partners
4.1 A person becomes a partner by applying on the Record, signing this agreement there with their drawn signature, giving their particulars under section 4.2, and being admitted: by joint resolution, or by accepting a personal invitation that the partners authorized, by joint resolution or through the Secretary under a grant they made. They make their first contribution once admitted. This replaces the consent of all partners that section 16401(i) would otherwise require.
4.2 Every partner must be at least 18 years old and able to contract. Each partner gives the officers their particulars: legal name, date of birth, residence address, email and tax status. The Partnership needs them for its accounts, its fictitious business name statement and its registrations. No partner gives a taxpayer identification number through the Record or the website; the Treasurer collects each partner's Form W-9 (or Form W-8BEN) separately when the tax returns require it. They are encrypted so that only the partner, the Treasurer and the Secretary can read them, kept off the Record, and corrected by the partner at any time. A partner may be known on the Record by a lived name.
4.3 Each partner keeps their sign-in key and password private and uses two-factor sign-in when the partners require it.

## 5. Capital and units
5.1 Contributions are in money. Each partner's interest is measured in units. A contribution buys units at the unit value on the valuation date at or after it is received.
5.2 The unit value is the Partnership's net assets divided by the units outstanding, set by the Treasurer at least monthly from the broker's prices and recorded on the Record.
5.3 Each partner has a capital account kept under Treasury Regulation section 1.704-1(b)(2)(iv). Profits, losses and every item of income, gain, deduction and credit are allocated in proportion to units.
5.4 A partnership certificate is issued from the Record when a partner's units change, and is shown only to the partner who holds it. It is evidence only: not a security, not negotiable, and transferable to no one.

## 6. Decisions
6.1 Each partner has one vote, and votes their units with it. Resolutions are decided on the Record, each in a single vote:
   - **joint**: a majority of the partners voting *and* of the units voting, at least half of each voting;
   - **joint-super**: two thirds of each;
   - **ordinary** (procedural matters only): a majority of votes cast, one fifth of partners voting.
6.2 Every purchase or sale of an investment is a joint resolution of the partners, stating the security, the most to buy or sell, any price limit and a deadline. This power is never delegated. The Treasurer places at the broker only what a resolution authorized, and records each trade.
6.3 Admitting or removing a partner, distributions, and electing or removing an officer are joint resolutions. Amending this agreement, selling all investments and dissolving are joint-super resolutions.

## 7. Participation
7.1 Every partner takes part: they study proposed investments, vote on resolutions, and attend meetings. A partner is in good standing while they have voted on at least three quarters of the last eight resolutions on which they could vote, and checked in to at least half of the last four meetings, as the Record shows. The partners may change these figures by organic resolution, but never to zero.
7.2 The first 30 days after admission do not count.
7.3 The Secretary gives written notice on the Record to a partner below the minimum. A partner still below it 30 days after notice may be removed by joint resolution, and is then redeemed under section 12.
7.4 A partner who cannot take part should withdraw under section 12.

## 8. Officers
8.1 The officers are the **Treasurer** and the **Secretary**, each elected directly by the partners on the Record for one year: every partner is a candidate unless they decline, and the partners vote by approval voting. One person may hold both offices. Until the first election, the founding partner serves as both. An officer may be removed, and a new election called, by joint resolution at any time.
8.2 **The Treasurer** keeps the brokerage and bank accounts in the Partnership's name and EIN only; places and records authorized trades; alone records contributions, trades, income, expenses, payouts and the monthly unit value on the books, which no other partner or officer may change; reconciles the books with the broker each month; collects each partner's Form W-9 off the Record; prepares or has prepared the federal Form 1065 and California Form 565 and every partner's Schedule K-1 by their due date (March 15 for a calendar year, or the extended date); and serves as the partnership representative under Internal Revenue Code section 6223. When eligible, the Partnership elects out of the centralized partnership audit regime (section 6221(b)).
8.3 **The Secretary** receives applications; sees that every partner signs this agreement before admission; keeps the roll, the minutes and the list of the Partnership's legal obligations; calls meetings; gives participation notices; signs checkpoints of the Record; and keeps the Partnership's registrations current (the fictitious business name, and any City of Berkeley business license the City requires).
8.4 No officer may contract for, or bind, the Partnership beyond what a resolution or a grant on the Record authorizes.

## 9. Privacy
9.1 The Partnership collects only the particulars in section 4.2. They are never put on the Record or its website; they are kept encrypted to the partner and the officers, shared only with the IRS, the Franchise Tax Board, the broker, the bank and the county clerk as the law or an account requires, and deleted when no longer needed for a return (at least three years after the partner's last return).
9.2 Partners keep the Partnership's business confidential, except as the law requires.
9.3 If personal information held by the Partnership is acquired by an unauthorized person, the officers notify the partners affected as Civil Code section 1798.82 requires.

## 10. Distributions
10.1 Distributions are made only by joint resolution, in proportion to units, in cash or in kind.
10.2 The Partnership does not join associations or grant anyone a claim on its profits or assets.

## 11. Books and information
11.1 The books are kept by the Treasurer alone. Every partner may read the Record, the books and the broker's statements at any time (Corporations Code section 16403); the books are open to every partner on the Partnership's website.

## 12. Withdrawal and redemption
12.1 A partner may withdraw by notice on the Record. A withdrawn or removed partner is redeemed at the unit value on the first valuation date after their withdrawal or removal, in cash or, by joint resolution, in kind, within 90 days. The Partnership continues among the others.

## 13. Liability
13.1 Each partner is personally liable for the Partnership's obligations, jointly and severally (Corporations Code section 16306). Among themselves, partners bear any loss in proportion to their units, and the Partnership indemnifies a partner who acted within this agreement and in good faith.
13.2 The duties of loyalty and care in Corporations Code section 16404 apply in full.

## 14. Compliance and disputes
14.1 The Partnership complies with federal, California and City of Berkeley law; the Secretary keeps a list of the obligations and who meets each.
14.2 Partners first try to resolve a dispute among themselves, then by mediation in Alameda County, before going to court. California law governs this agreement; the courts of Alameda County have jurisdiction.

## 15. Amendment and dissolution
15.1 This agreement is amended by joint-super resolution setting out the new text, proposed by any partner on the Partnership's website. No amendment may, without a partner's written consent, require more capital from them, increase their liability, or take away their rights under sections 11 and 12.
15.2 The Partnership dissolves by joint-super resolution or when fewer than two partners remain. The Treasurer then sells its investments, pays its obligations, files its final returns and statements, and distributes the rest in proportion to capital accounts.

*Signed on the Record by each partner, with the date of signing.*
