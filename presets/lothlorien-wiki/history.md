Lothlórien, usually called Loth, is a student housing cooperative of the Berkeley Student Cooperative. It occupies two former mansions at 2405 and 2415 Prospect Street in Berkeley's Southside neighborhood, joined by a courtyard [1][9]. The buildings are much older than the co-op: the North House dates from the 1880s and the South House from 1906. The co-op bought them in 1975 and opened Lothlórien that year as its first all-vegetarian house [1][2]. This page follows the houses from their construction to the present. Where two sources disagree, the page says so and gives both.

## Terms used on this page
- **BSC** (Berkeley Student Cooperative): the nonprofit student housing cooperative that owns Lothlórien and about twenty other houses and apartment buildings. It was founded in 1933 and was called the University of California Students' Cooperative Association (UCSCA), then the USCA, before it took its present name in 2007 [2].
- **North House** and **South House**: 2405 Prospect Street and 2415 Prospect Street [1].
- **Council**: the weekly meeting of all members, which makes the house's decisions by consensus. See the page on council and consensus.
- **Elves**: the name residents use for themselves, after the elves of Lothlórien in J. R. R. Tolkien's *The Lord of the Rings* [1].
- **Retrofit**: structural work that strengthens a building so it is more likely to survive an earthquake.

## The North House, 1880s to 1903
![The North House as George H. Maxwell's residence, before it was moved to Prospect Street](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Maxwell_House_1895.png "The Maxwell house, now the North House, as printed in 1895. From William Carey Jones, Illustrated History of the University of California (1895). Public domain; Wikimedia Commons.")

The North House was built in the 1880s for George Hebard Maxwell, a lawyer and water-rights advocate who later helped shape the National Reclamation Act of 1902. The architect was Clinton Day [1]. The year is uncertain: the current Wikipedia article gives 1885, and an earlier revision of the same article gave 1883 [1][3].

The house did not start on Prospect Street. In 1903 Fred and Alice Clark, a real estate developer and his wife, bought it from the Maxwell family and moved it about two blocks east, up the hill, to its present lot. The lumber millionaire O. S. Johnson owned it later [1].

## The South House, 1906
![Phillip G. Galpin's residence at 2415 Prospect Street](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Galpin_mansion.png "The Galpin residence, now the South House, in an early newspaper photograph. Public domain; Wikimedia Commons.")

The South House was built in 1906 for Phillip Galpin, an attorney [1].

## Fraternities and sororities, 1920s to 1960s
Both houses were used by fraternities and sororities until the 1960s [1]. An earlier revision of the Wikipedia article, which cites the *Oakland Tribune* of those years, gives more detail. In 1924 the North House became the founding chapter of the Chi Pi Sigma fraternity, and the fraternity had left by 1940, possibly in 1937 over unpaid rent. The South House was home to a series of sororities from 1927; the last of them, Delta Sigma Epsilon, closed in 1951 [3]. A fraternity lived in the South House after the sororities [1].

## The One World Family, 1973 to 1975
The One World Family Commune was founded in San Francisco in 1967 by Allen Noonan, who took the name Allen Michael in 1970. Its teachings mixed UFO beliefs, Christian themes, New Age spirituality and what it called "Uni-Communism." Members held their income in common, drew no personal wages, and worked a daily five-hour shift they called "love service" [4]. In Berkeley the commune ran the One World Family Natural Foods and Entertainment Center at Telegraph Avenue and Haste Street, with a restaurant, a bakery, a pizzeria, an airbrushed clothing store and a performance hall [4]. A 2015 history on FoundSF says the restaurant served 200 to 300 people a day and that the commune's family council made decisions by consensus [5].

In early 1973 the commune moved into 2405 and 2415 Prospect Street, which it named Novida and Altamira. At its peak that year it had 55 adults and 25 children living in Berkeley [4]. The Wikipedia article on Lothlórien says the commune's communal and artistic ways influenced the culture of the co-op that followed it [1]. In 1975 the commune left Berkeley for Stockton [4].

The sources tell the handover in two ways. The Wikipedia article on the commune says it sold the two houses to the co-op before it left [4]. The FoundSF history says the commune rented the houses, was reportedly unable to pay its rent, and that the USCA bought the houses in 1975 [5].

## The co-op opens, 1975
The USCA opened Lothlórien in 1975 as its first all-vegetarian house [2]. The name comes from the elven forest in Tolkien's novel, and residents have called themselves elves since [1]. Two customs that sources describe as long-standing are an annual initiation for new members and a party called the Food Orgy, at which people make trays of finger food and feed one another without utensils [1][3].

## 1984
In 1984 Roberta "Bibi" Lee, a former resident, disappeared while jogging in the Oakland hills. Her body was found five weeks later. Her boyfriend, Bradley Page, was convicted of manslaughter at a retrial in 1988 [1]. The narrow top-floor room where she lived is known in the house as the Spirit Room [1].

## Activism and the oak grove, 2006 to 2008
Members joined the protests over the oak grove beside the university's Memorial Stadium, where a tree-sit lasted from December 2006 to September 2008 [3]. The same source says the house organized and passed a campus referendum that created an annual fund of $100,000 for green projects at the university [3].

## Retrofits and solar, 2009 to 2013
Lothlórien sits on the Hayward Fault, which runs past its back yard [6]. The BSC rated the North House as high risk, and it was retrofitted in the summer of 2009 with money from the BSC's retrofit bonds [6]. Alfred Twu, a resident at the time, put the cost at $537,000, about $19,000 per resident, financed by a 40-year bond [7]. One page of the 2013 alumni newsletter says the work happened "three summers ago," which would be 2010, so the year is not settled [6].

In September 2010 a 7.92-kilowatt solar array went onto the roof of the South House, and it began producing power on December 1, 2010. It cost $39,611. The money came from a five-year loan of $24,000 from the BSC's central office at 5.25 percent, $13,266 from House Approved Projects, and $2,345 raised by members [8]. Lothlórien was the first BSC house to install photovoltaic solar panels [1].

The South House was retrofitted in the summer of 2013. The work closed its kitchen, and residents received a 33 percent rent rebate that brought rent to about $350 a month. About a third of the residents took their meals at other houses, among them Wolf, Oscar Wilde, Davis and Castro, and the African-American Theme House shared its kitchen and laundry. Council met every other week that summer, and workshift was assigned by area of the house instead of by time, since there were no dishes to wash [6].

## Lothlórien now
As of fall 2026 the BSC lists Lothlórien with 58 residents and describes it as two old Victorian homes joined by a courtyard. The BSC page says the house practices participatory democracy, consent and anti-oppression, focuses on uplifting BIPOC, queer and trans members, and holds a weekly council in the North House living room [9]. The house constitution, last updated in May 2026, describes the house in one line [10]:

> Lothlórien is "a vegetarian, consent-and-consensus-based cooperative."

In February 2026 council adopted a procedure for passing motions by reaction in the house Slack, the house's group chat [10]. The constitution also lists a BIPOC Community and Experience Manager among the elected positions [10].
