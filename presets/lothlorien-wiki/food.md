Lothlórien has been the BSC's vegetarian house since it opened in 1975, when the co-op described it as its first all-vegetarian house [1]. This page collects what the house rules and the BSC say about food, the kitchen and eating together [2][3][4].

## Terms used on this page
- **Vegetarian**: food without meat, poultry or fish.
- **House funds**: money held by the house, as distinct from a member's own money.
- **Common space**: any part of the house that is not a member's own room.
- **Late plate**: a plate of dinner set aside for a member who cannot be there when it is served [3].
- **Boarder**: a member who eats at the house and does workshift there without living there.
- **Karma box**: the house's box for contributions such as guests' dinner money [3].

## The vegetarian rule
The constitution's bylaws say that no meat may be bought with house funds [2]. A member may keep and cook their own meat, under these rules [2][4]:
- meat is stored properly, as the BSC's house page puts it;
- meat is prepared only with separate equipment kept for it, and only on the red cutting boards;
- cast iron pans and wooden cutting boards are never used for meat.

All meals cooked for the house are vegetarian [4]. Wikipedia describes the bylaw as banning the preparing, storing or eating of meat in common space, which is stricter than the May 2026 text above. It also says that keeping a vegetarian diet is not a condition of living in the house [5].

## Ordering and the kitchen
The Kitchen Manager orders the house's food, oversees food safety and keeps the kitchen cleaning schedule. They must earn ServSafe Manager certification, a food safety qualification, by the end of week 3 and submit a kitchen budget by the start of week 4 [2]. Members can ask for particular foods and spices through the request system the BSC page calls CFS, and the house tries to stock a range of ingredients for the different food cultures of its members [4]. The house cooks around allergies with alternatives such as gluten-free pizza or a nut-free dinner [4].

## Keeping the kitchen clean
The community agreements ask everyone to wash, rinse and rack their dishes, put food away, date anything they open and close the fridge [3]. Kitchen speakers stay off during quiet hours, except that on Saturday the brunch shift may turn them on from 9 a.m. [3]. Quiet hours run from 10 p.m. to 10 a.m. Sunday through Thursday and from 1 a.m. to 10 a.m. on Friday and Saturday [3].

## Late plates
The last people in the dinner line make up the late plates and label each one with the name of the member it is for. A late plate is held for two hours after dinner begins or is announced. After that anyone may eat it, and whoever does should say so in the dinner thread so the member who asked for it knows [3].

## Guests at dinner
A dinner guest pays the daily boarder minimum into the karma box, or their host pays it. Dinner guests give $3 to $5 on a sliding scale, and they wait until the residents have been served [3].

## Special dinners and the Food Orgy
To attend a Special Dinner a member must be no more than 2 hours down on workshift and have done at least 2 humor hours and 2 hab hours; the workshift page explains these terms [3][6]. The Food Orgy is a party at which people make trays of finger food and feed one another, with no utensils and nobody feeding themselves [5].
