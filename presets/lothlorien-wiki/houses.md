Lothlórien is two buildings on Prospect Street in Berkeley: the North House at 2405 and the South House at 2415, joined by a courtyard [1][2]. Both are three stories, and together they hold about 26,000 square feet [3]. This page describes the buildings, the rooms people live in, the shared spaces and the grounds as the sources describe them. For how the buildings came to be here, see the history page.

## Terms used on this page
- **Single, double, triple, quad**: a bedroom for one, two, three or four people.
- **Dingle**: a former double now let as a single. The word appears in the 2013 alumni newsletter, when doubles were let as singles during the South House retrofit [4].
- **Retrofit**: structural work that strengthens a building against earthquakes.
- **Photovoltaic (PV) panels**: solar panels that make electricity, as distinct from solar thermal panels that heat water.

## The North House
![The North House in 1895](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Maxwell_House_1895.png "The North House in 1895, when it stood on Piedmont Way. Public domain; Wikimedia Commons.")

The North House was built in the 1880s to a design by the architect Clinton Day and was moved to its present lot in 1903 [2]. Its living room is the house's main room for social events, movie nights and the weekly council [1]. The constitution also names the North House living room as the place council meets [5]. The North House library is the largest library in the BSC according to the BSC's house page [1]. The community agreements keep the library free of substances outside of socials [6].

## The South House
![The South House, early 1900s](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Galpin_mansion.png "The South House as the Galpin residence. Public domain; Wikimedia Commons.")

The South House was built in 1906 [2]. Its kitchen is where dinner is cooked; when the 2013 retrofit closed it, the house kept a toaster, a microwave and a coffeemaker going and the kitchen stayed the social center of the house [4]. In 2011 the two houses used about 125 kilowatt-hours of electricity a day, 100 of them in the South House. Refrigeration took about 60 and the hot tub about 20 [3]. The South House is the only one of the two with wheelchair access, through a back door, and as of 2011 neither house had an accessible bathroom [3].

## Shared spaces
The BSC's house page lists these features [1]:
- a library, a band room and an art studio, with two pianos;
- a fireplace;
- a sauna, a hot tub and a roof deck;
- a sundeck with laundry lines;
- a treehouse, a courtyard, gardens and a front garden;
- bike repair tools, 40 bike spaces and one parking space.

The community agreements add a few rules for these spaces. The study room, the kitchen and the projector wall in the North House living room should not be painted over, and any new mural, or the removal of one, needs council approval [6]. Anyone using the sauna should learn how it works first, check the rocks before turning it on, keep the heat moderate and switch it off afterward [6].

## The grounds and the fault
The Hayward Fault runs past the back yard [4]. The North House was retrofitted in 2009 at a cost of $537,000 and the South House in the summer of 2013; the history page has the details and the disagreement over the North House year [3][4]. The same 2011 account puts the cost of retrofitting the treehouse at about $700 in materials [3].

The house's own garden grows well under one percent of what the house eats, so as of 2011 residents also worked a community garden about fifteen blocks away [3]. A Garden Manager, elected each semester, looks after outdoor habitability, fire safety, native plantings and garden education [5].

## Solar
A 7.92-kilowatt grid-tied solar array sits on the roof of the South House. It was built in September 2010 by the installer Alter Systems and began producing power on December 1, 2010. It uses 33 modules of 240 watts each and a 7-kilowatt inverter, and it cost $39,611 in all [7]. In 2011 it produced 3 to 28 kilowatt-hours a day in winter and 40 to 55 in summer [3]. Lothlórien was the first co-op house to install photovoltaic panels [2].
