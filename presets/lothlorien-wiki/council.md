Council is Lothlórien's legislative body: the meeting of the whole house that adopts policy, approves spending and elects the managers. It decides by consensus. Lothlórien is the only BSC house that uses consensus in place of majority vote [1]. This page sets out how council works under the house constitution as last updated in May 2026 [2]. Where this page and the constitution differ, the constitution governs, and where the constitution is silent the house follows BSC policy [2].

## Terms used on this page
- **Member**: anyone living or boarding in the house under a BSC contract [2].
- **Boarder**: a member who eats at the house and does workshift there without living there.
- **Quorum**: the number of members who must be present for council to make a binding decision.
- **Consensus**: a way of deciding in which the meeting works to answer every member's concerns instead of counting a majority. Wikipedia describes Lothlórien's practice as Quaker-based [1].
- **Minor objection**: a serious concern that cannot be compromised on. A personal preference does not count as one [2].
- **Major objection**: an objection so serious that the person raising it would consider moving out if the motion passed [2].
- **Coord**: the house's short word for a manager, as in policy coord or hab coord.

## Meetings
Council meets every Sunday at 7 p.m. in the North House living room unless members are told otherwise [2]. Quorum is 25 percent of the house membership, and no binding vote can happen without it [2]. The House President, also called the Policy Coord, presides and sets the agenda 24 hours ahead; the House Manager presides when the President cannot [2]. A special meeting needs 24 hours' written notice from the President or a petition signed by a quorum [2]. Any spending over $150 from the house checking account needs council's approval first [2].

## How a motion passes
A motion is blocked by one major objection or by three minor objections [2]. Under the house's "Who Consenses?" policy a motion passes when it has no major objection, two or fewer minor objections, and about half of the people present actively consenting. Anyone who objected is then asked to explain why [2].

The Consensus Caveat Policy, passed on March 15, 2009, deals with a deadlock. If someone raises a major objection, another member may counter it with an objection just as serious, and the meeting then works toward consensus for 15 more minutes. If it is still stuck, two thirds of the members may vote to move to a vote, and the motion then needs five sixths of those present to pass [2].

## Motions in Slack
Since February 15, 2026, a motion can also pass in the house Slack, the house's group chat, by emoji reaction [2]:
- a thumbs-up means yes, and the first two are the proposer and the seconder;
- a thumbs-down means no;
- a boxed cross means a minor objection;
- a red cross means a major objection;
- a question mark asks for the motion to wait for discussion at council.

A Slack motion can take effect 48 hours after it was posted or last amended. It passes when 25 percent of the house has consented and nobody has raised a major objection, a minor objection or a call for council discussion. A member who does not react is counted as abstaining. A minor objection sends the motion to the next council, and the policy coord can require discussion of any motion [2].

## Emergency council and emergency motions
An emergency council needs the House Manager's approval. It should meet at dinner or whenever the most people are home, ideally with 24 hours' notice, and its quorum is 12 people, or 6 outside the contract period [2]. The community agreements add an emergency motion that only the Policy Coordinator may declare. It has to be urgent, unable to wait for council and temporary. Objections go privately to the Policy Coordinator; one major objection pauses the motion for mediation and three minor objections block it. Every emergency motion lapses when the next council begins [3].

## Managers
Managers are elected each semester and answer to council. Each one reports at every council, attends the weekly managers' meeting and completes the trainings the job requires [2]. The constitution lists these positions and the weekly workshift hours each one carries [2]:
| Position | Hours a week | Duties |
|---|:-:|---|
| House President (Policy Coord) | 5 | runs council and elections, oversees the bylaws and votes of confidence, and supervises the other managers |
| House Manager | 5 | move-ins, room bids, move-outs, the house budget and parking |
| Workshift Manager | 5 | assigns workshift, posts the permanent schedule by Monday of week 3, and enforces the workshift policy |
| Kitchen Manager | 5 | food ordering, food safety and kitchen cleaning; must earn ServSafe Manager certification, a food safety qualification, by the end of week 3 |
| Maintenance Manager | 5 | repairs, tools and supplies, room condition reports and fire safety inspections |
| Waste Reduction Manager | 5 | trash, recycling and special recycling |
| Habitability Manager (Hab Coord) | 5 | habitability training, self-inspections and the list of improvement projects. Habitability is the BSC's word for keeping the house clean and fit to live in |
| Health Manager | 3 | a monthly health sheet, one outreach event a month, and CPR and first aid training |
| Network Manager | 2 | the house network and projector loans |
| Finance Manager | 2 | the house's financial records, bills, deposits and the monthly reconciliation of the account |
| Social Manager | none | socials, social hours and the BSC party policy; facilitates the Social Anarchist Council |
| Garden Manager | none | outdoor habitability, fire safety, native plantings and garden education |
| BIPOC Community and Experience Manager | 5 | duties to be set by the community |
| BSC Board Representative | 5 | attends the BSC Board and reports back to the house |

No regular workshift is listed for the Social or Garden Manager.

Five positions share the house's managerial pay: Workshift 32 percent, Kitchen 24 percent, Maintenance 16 percent, House President 14 percent and House Manager 14 percent. The others are unpaid, and two people holding one job split its share [2].

## Elections, confidence and recall
The policy coord runs elections each semester by anonymous online poll. A candidate needs a majority yes vote and is disqualified by one major objection or three minor objections [2]. Each contract period has two rounds of votes of confidence, the first by week 7 and the second by week 12. A simple majority of "no confidence" in the first round requires the manager to follow a corrective action plan. One major objection or three minor objections about a manager's core duties start a recall [2]. A recall hearing is held at the next council, and removal takes an absolute majority, meaning more than half of all current residents, voting yes on an anonymous ballot [2]. The constitution gives week 12 for the second round in one place and week 15 in the position descriptions, so check with the policy coord [2].
